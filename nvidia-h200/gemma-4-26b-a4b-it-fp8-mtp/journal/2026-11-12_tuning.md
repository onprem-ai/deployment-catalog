# 2026-11-12 - Tuning on H200

**Goal:** Better settings for one H200 within a 4 h budget shared with the 31B card (start
21:51:57Z), then the full benchmark once.

Probe as in the initial entry: the skill probe on the node, straight to the engine, thinking off;
~250-token prompts with 512-token answers; ~5,300-token prompts with 64-token answers for prefill;
20 s warm-up, 40 s window. Each round is committed, deployed fresh and undeployed.

Round 1: vLLM's `num_speculative_tokens_per_batch_size` with 0 draft tokens for 1 to 4 requests
made one user **slower** (93 against 145 tokens/s), not faster: the drafter still runs in every
step and the steps keep their shape. The schedule cannot switch the drafter off by load. 256
sequences raised throughput at 256 requests to 11,089 tokens/s (KV cache 88 % full), within the
48 GiB engine booking; the GPU held 52.7 GiB, above the 50 GiB hardware booking.

Round 2: without the drafter one user gets 183 tokens/s, but the throughput under load falls far
behind (5,972 against 9,430 tokens/s at 128). The KV cache is also smaller without the drafter
(519,572 against 799,873 tokens from the same 17.8 GiB) and is full at 256 requests. The drafter
stays, with 1 draft token.

Round 3: the drafter back with 256 sequences, 16,384 batched tokens and 56 GiB of engine booking:
11,406 tokens/s at 256 requests (+17 % over round 0's 9,752 at 128), the KV cache only 62 % full
(726,486 tokens). The GPU held 60.9 GiB; one user stays at 142 tokens/s. 16,384 batched tokens did
not change prefill (8,999 against 9,259 tokens/s at 1; 52,707 at 16 as before).

Round 4: 512 sequences gave nothing at 256 requests (11,385) and less at 512 (9,688 tokens/s, time to
first token 1.3 s), and the GPU rose to 84.0 GiB: with that many sequences vLLM's CUDA graphs and
sampler buffers grow far past the engine booking. 256 sequences stay.

## Chosen

Round 3: 1 draft token, 256 sequences, 16,384 batched tokens, engine booking 56 GiB
(`--gpu-memory-utilization 0.4006`), hardware booking 62 GiB (the round 3 peak was 60.9 GiB).
Against the start: about the same single-user speed (142 against 141 to 150 tokens/s), the same at
128 requests (9,358 against 9,752), and 256 requests at once now run at 11,406 tokens/s.
