# 2026-11-11 - Tuning on H200

**Goal:** Find the settings for Arete 27B on one H200 with short probes, then run the full
benchmark once.

## How it was measured

The probe ran on the inference node, straight to the engine on the replica's network: about
276-token prompts with 512-token answers at 1, 32 and 128 requests in flight, and
6,000-token prompts with 64-token answers at 1 and 16 for prefill; a 40 s window after 20 s
of warm-up, reasoning off (`chat_template_kwargs: {"enable_thinking": false}`). Each round
changed one setting, committed to this catalog before the redeploy. The engine's scheduler
lines (running and waiting requests, KV cache use) and its DFlash acceptance length were read
from the replica's log over SSH.

## Answers

Through the API gateway: 17 x 23 and the prime count right with reasoning off and at every
effort (`low`, `medium`, `xhigh`; the template knows no `none` or `high`, and answers 400 for
them, naming the three it knows); two parallel tool calls and a correct answer from their
results; an image answered; `/v1/messages`.

## Start-up

`Selected FlashInferFp8DeepGEMMDynamicBlockScaledKernel for Fp8PerBlockOnlineLinearMethod`;
`Online quantization ... took 12.06 seconds`; `Model loading took 32.17 GiB` (FP8 target and the
BF16 drafter). The DFlash2 drafter takes text only ("does not support external multimodal
embeddings"). The engine is ready about 9 minutes after the deploy, most of it FlashInfer's
JIT compilation of the linear-attention prefill (vLLM: "Set --gdn-prefill-backend triton to
skip JIT"), which the container loses on every redeploy.

## What the rounds show

- **The KV cache, not compute, limits the replica at 128 requests.** At 128 in flight the
  engine ran only 53 requests with 75 waiting and the KV cache at 97.6 to 99.5 percent
  (rounds 1 and 2); the waiting requests make the 16 s time to first token. Arete is a hybrid
  model (48 linear-attention layers, 16 full-attention layers): vLLM aligns the attention block
  to the linear-attention state and sets it to 1,664 tokens ("Setting attention block size to
  1664 tokens"), so every running request holds at least one 1,664-token block, and its
  recurrent state (about 75 MiB) is kept once per draft position under speculative decoding.
  The 1.6M tokens of KV cache therefore hold far fewer requests than their size suggests.
- The batch size and prefix caching do not change this (rounds 1 and 2: within the noise).
  Prefix caching is switched back on, so repeated prompts are not prefilled again.
- Shorter drafts free state per request: 4 draft tokens ran 84 requests at once (round 3),
  and 128 GiB of engine booking 102 (round 4). Both raise the throughput at 128 and cut the
  time to first token, at about the same single-user speed.
- The DFlash2 drafter was trained on Qwen3.8-27B, not on Arete; its mean acceptance is 2.7 to
  2.9 tokens, lower than on Muse Glimmer or Ripple with their own drafters.

## Round 5 and a preemption

Round 5 set the draft length by batch size (7 tokens up to 8 requests, 4 above). The spot VM
was preempted at about 15:35Z while it started; it was started again and came back on another
physical GPU (`GPU-0666bb44-272e-e2a7-cb48-9a3267a4bfb8`). The replica failed with
`inference-service-replica-gpu-unavailable` (error report `01a1cb4b-f77f-78ba-b176-e827a0cc08a4`)
and was moved to the new GPU with `set_replicas`; the weights were still on the node's disk.

| Round | Change | c=1 | c=8 | c=32 | c=128 | TTFT at 128 | Acceptance |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | draft length by batch size, 7 then 4 | 180 | 1,245 | 2,632 | 2,823 | 11.4 s | 2.77 |

It lost: no gain alone (180 against 177 in round 4, within the noise), and at 128 only 65
requests ran with 64 waiting at 98.8 percent KV cache. The engine reserves the state for the
longest draft in the schedule, so the schedule costs the room that 4 fixed tokens free.

## Chosen

Round 4: DFlash2 with 4 draft tokens, 128 sequences, 16,384 batched tokens, prefix caching on,
FP8 KV cache, 262,144 tokens of context, 128 GiB engine booking, 132 GiB hardware booking.
Against the starting settings: about the same single-user speed (177 against 185 tokens/s),
41 percent more throughput at 128 (3,337 against 2,365) and a quarter of the time to first
token at 128 (3.9 s against 16.1 s).
