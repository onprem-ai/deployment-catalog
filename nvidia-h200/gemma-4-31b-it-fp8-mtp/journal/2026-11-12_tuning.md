# 2026-11-12 - Tuning on H200

**Goal:** Better settings for one H200 within a 4 h budget shared with the 26B-A4B card (start
21:51:57Z), then the full benchmark once.

Probe as in the initial entry: the skill probe on the node, straight to the engine, thinking off;
~250-token prompts with 512-token answers; ~5,300-token prompts with 64-token answers for prefill;
20 s warm-up, 40 s window. Each round is committed, deployed fresh and undeployed.

The initial entry's rounds found: 4 draft tokens best for one user (108 to 116 tokens/s), 2 draft
tokens 4 to 9 percent better at 32 and 128 requests; at 128 requests the KV cache was 94 to 100 %
full with 72 GiB of engine booking.

Round 1: vLLM's `num_speculative_tokens_per_batch_size` with 4 draft tokens for up to 8 requests and
2 above keeps one user at 4-token speed (106 tokens/s) and gives the 2-token throughput under load
(2,757 at 32, 3,787 at 128; acceptance 2.22). Kept. The KV cache is still 90 % full at 128.

Round 2: 92 GiB of engine booking raised the KV cache memory from 34.6 to 53.9 GiB, but the cache
holds about the same number of tokens (394,430 against 389,156): the 31B model mixes sliding-window
layers (256-wide heads, 1,024 tokens) with full-attention layers (512-wide heads), and vLLM sizes the
pool by the larger groups. Throughput did not change (3,804 at 128 against 3,787; 4,053 at 256 with
2.35 s to the first token): the dense 31B model is bound by compute at this load, not by KV cache.
Not kept; the GPU held 93.9 GiB for nothing.

## Chosen

Round 1: draft length by load (4 draft tokens up to 8 requests, 2 above), 128 sequences, 8,192
batched tokens, engine booking 72 GiB, hardware booking 74 GiB. Against the start: one user about
the same (106 against 108 to 116 tokens/s), +10 % at 32 requests (2,757 against 2,498), +4 % at 128
(3,787 against 3,628).
