# 2026-11-13 - Tuning on H200

Probe: the skill probe on the node, straight to the engine, thinking off (`enable_thinking: false`);
~275-token prompts with 512-token answers; ~6,000-token prompts with 64-token answers for prefill;
20 s warm-up, 40 s window. Each round is committed, deployed fresh and undeployed.

Round 0: `Model loading took 29.02 GiB`, `Available KV cache memory: 92.94 GiB`, `GPU KV cache size:
2,579,669 tokens`, CUDA graph pool 1.98 GiB, `init engine ... took 285.02 s`. Against Arete with
DFlash2 (177 / 2,580 / 3,337 tokens/s at 1 / 32 / 128): Qwen's own MTP head is 15 % faster for one user
and 36 % faster at 128 requests.

Round 1: the schedule gains 4 % for one user (213 against 204) but loses 6 % at 32 and 12 % at 128
(3,980 against 4,548), and at 128 the KV cache was full with 21 requests waiting (1.9 s to the first
token): in this hybrid model vLLM keeps the linear-attention state once per draft position, sized for
the schedule's longest draft (4), so fewer requests fit than with 3. The schedule loses.

Round 2: 2 draft tokens lose 7 % for one user (190 against 204) and gain 4 % at 128 (4,734 against
4,548), with the KV cache only 71 % full at 128. 3 draft tokens stay: the better single-user speed,
and the difference under load is small.

Round 3: 256 sequences give nothing: at 256 requests the KV cache is full with 119 requests waiting
(15 s to the first token) and throughput falls to 4,275. The 128 GiB engine booking holds about 137
requests of this length; more needs a second replica.

## Chosen

Round 0, the Arete settings with Qwen's MTP head: 3 draft tokens, 128 sequences, 16,384 batched tokens,
engine booking 128 GiB, hardware booking 136 GiB (peaks 131.2 to 133.2 GiB in the rounds; 134,583 MiB
with the long prompts in round 0 is 131.4 GiB). Not tried within the session: a smaller engine booking
to share the GPU, `--language-model-only` for text-only use (frees the vision encoder's memory).
